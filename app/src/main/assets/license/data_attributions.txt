DICTATE — THIRD-PARTY DATA & MODEL ATTRIBUTIONS
==============================================

Dictate bundles and/or downloads language data and neural models from the following
freely licensed sources. Of the language corpora, only aggregate frequency data (word
and word-pair counts) is redistributed — never the underlying source texts. Every model
listed here runs on your device; none of them send audio or text anywhere.


1. Word-frequency dictionaries (glide typing, autocorrect, spell check)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Files: ime/dict/<lang>.json  (bundled English; other languages downloaded on demand)

Derived from the OPUS OpenSubtitles frequency lists.
  - OPUS — the open parallel corpus: https://opus.nlpl.eu
  - Please cite: P. Lison & J. Tiedemann (2016), "OpenSubtitles2016: Extracting
    Large Parallel Corpora from Movie and TV Subtitles", LREC 2016.
For Hindi, Tamil and Urdu, where subtitle data alone is too sparse, word counts from
the Leipzig Corpora Collection (CC BY, cited in section 2 below) are merged in.
Casing was restored, and corpus noise filtered out, using Hunspell dictionaries as a
build-time oracle only (github.com/wooorm/dictionaries and, for Arabic and the Indic
languages, github.com/LibreOffice/dictionaries); their word lists are not redistributed.


2. Bigram (word-pair) frequency data for autocorrect context (Tier 2)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Files: ime/dict/<lang>_bigrams.txt  (bundled English; other languages downloaded on demand)

Derived from the Leipzig Corpora Collection (Wortschatz), used under CC BY.
  - © Universität Leipzig / Sächsische Akademie der Wissenschaften / InfAI
  - https://wortschatz-leipzig.de
  - Please cite: D. Goldhahn, T. Eckart & U. Quasthoff (2012), "Building Large
    Monolingual Dictionaries at the Leipzig Corpora Collection: From 100 to 200
    Languages", LREC 2012.
  - License: Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY) — the downloadable corpus
    packages are CC BY (the online query service is CC BY-NC and is not used here).


3. On-device speech-to-text models (offline transcription)
----------------------------------------------------------
Downloaded on demand into <filesDir>/dictate-models/<model>/ ; no audio ever leaves the device.

One-shot models — these transcribe after you stop speaking:

  - Whisper (tiny/base/small, multilingual and English) — © OpenAI, MIT License.
    https://github.com/openai/whisper
  - Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 — © NVIDIA, licensed CC BY 4.0.
    https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3
  - Parakeet German (primeline) — a German-specialized fine-tune of NVIDIA Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3,
    © primeline, licensed CC BY 4.0. https://huggingface.co/primeline/parakeet-primeline

Streaming models — these transcribe live, while you are still speaking:

  - Kroko ASR community models (German, English, Spanish, French, Italian, Dutch, Portuguese,
    Swedish, Turkish, Hebrew) — © Banafo, licensed CC BY-SA.
    https://huggingface.co/Banafo/Kroko-ASR
    Redistributed unmodified. The ShareAlike term governs adaptations of the model itself; the
    files shipped here are verbatim copies.

4. Supporting on-device models
-------------------------------

  - Silero VAD — detects speech, used to segment long recordings and to skip silent ones.
    © Silero Team, MIT License. https://github.com/snakers4/silero-vad
  - Smart Turn v3 — decides whether a spoken thought is finished, for long-form auto-split.
    © Pipecat AI, licensed BSD-2-Clause. https://github.com/pipecat-ai/smart-turn

5. Model runtime and conversions
---------------------------------

The ONNX (int8) exports of every model above, and the on-device inference runtime, come from the
sherpa-onnx / k2-fsa project, Apache-2.0. https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx
Inference runs through ONNX Runtime, © Microsoft, MIT License. https://onnxruntime.ai

All of the above permit redistribution; the files are mirrored on the project's own GitHub release
for a stable, project-controlled source.

6. Emoji names and keywords (emoji search, emoji suggestions)
-------------------------------------------------------------
Files: ime/media/emoji/annotations/<lang>.txt  (bundled, ~50 languages)

The searchable name and keywords of every emoji, in each language, come from the Unicode Common
Locale Data Repository (CLDR v48), both the curated and the derived annotation sets.
  - © Unicode, Inc., licensed under the Unicode License v3 (SPDX: Unicode-3.0).
    https://github.com/unicode-org/cldr-json — https://www.unicode.org/license.txt
  - Regenerate with tools/emoji-annotations/generate.py

7. Provider logos
------------------

The settings list marks each AI provider with its own logo, so a row can be found by its mark rather
than read. The marks are used only to identify the service the row configures; every trademark
remains the property of its owner, and their use here implies no affiliation or endorsement.

The monochrome outlines come from two collections, both freely licensed:

  - Simple Icons, CC0-1.0 — OpenAI, OpenRouter, Google Gemini, Anthropic, Mistral AI, ElevenLabs,
    Deepgram, DeepSeek, Ollama. https://github.com/simple-icons/simple-icons
  - Lobe Icons, MIT License — Groq, Together AI, DeepInfra, AssemblyAI, xAI, SiliconFlow (filed
    there under SiliconCloud, the name of their platform).
    https://github.com/lobehub/lobe-icons

Soniox publish no vector mark, so theirs was traced from their own app icon and fitted to the same
size as the rest.
